Maternal Health Crisis Statistics

Before we talk about abortion, we have to talk about what's happening to mothers in America — because the two cannot be separated. The United States has the highest maternal mortality rate among high-income nations, and Mississippi consistently ranks among the worst states in the country.

32.9

maternal deaths per 100,000 live births (U.S., 2021 — CDC)

2–3×

Black women die from pregnancy-related causes at 2–3 times the rate of white women

84%

Mississippi's numbers are among the most alarming in the nation. These aren't statistics. They are mothers who did not come home. See the full Maternal Health Crisis page →

Section 02

The Roe Era (1973–2022)

On January 22, 1973, the U.S. Supreme Court decided Roe v. Wade — establishing a federal right to abortion and reshaping American law, culture, and medicine for nearly five decades.

63M+

estimated abortions performed in the U.S. between 1973 and 2022 (Guttmacher Institute)

49 years

Roe was the law of the land before it was overturned in 2022

29.3

abortions per 1,000 women — the peak rate in 1981 (Guttmacher Institute)

During the Roe era, abortion rates declined steadily from their 1981 peak as access to contraception expanded and cultural attitudes shifted. Even so, abortion remained one of the most common medical procedures performed in the United States. Planned Parenthood became the single largest abortion provider in the country, performing over 350,000 procedures annually by the end of the era.

Section 03

The Post-Dobbs Era (2022–Present)

On June 24, 2022, the Supreme Court decided Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization — the case built around Mississippi's own Gestational Age Act, a 15-week abortion ban passed by the Mississippi Legislature. The Court overturned Roe v. Wade, returning the authority to regulate abortion to individual states.

"I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made; your works are wonderful, I know that full well."Psalm 139:14
14+

states with near-total abortion bans as of 2024

~0

in-clinic abortions performed in Mississippi following the trigger law taking effect

57%

of all U.S. abortions in 2023 were medication-based — the dominant method post-Dobbs

Mississippi's trigger law took effect immediately after Dobbs, banning nearly all abortions in the state. Women seeking abortion began traveling to neighboring states or turning to mail-order medication abortion. Organizations like Aid Access and Plan C began shipping mifepristone and misoprostol to women in restricted states — a practice that generated significant legal debate.

Section 04

Self-Managed Abortion Statistics

A self-managed abortion is one that takes place outside of a clinical setting — typically using mifepristone and misoprostol (the "abortion pill" regimen) obtained online, by mail, or from an informal source. Since Dobbs, self-managed abortion has increased significantly in states with restrictions.

10 wks

FDA-approved window for mifepristone use (70 days gestation)

2–4%

rate of serious complications in supervised medication abortion (hemorrhage, infection)

↑

Pill shipments to restricted states rose significantly following the Dobbs decision

While medication abortion is considered safe when used in a supervised clinical setting, self-managed protocols without medical oversight carry elevated risks — including undetected ectopic pregnancy (a potentially fatal condition), hemorrhage requiring hospitalization, and incomplete abortion. Women who have begun the medication abortion process and have second thoughts have options. See the next section.

Section 05

Abortion Reversal Pill.

If you have taken the first abortion pill (mifepristone) and have changed your mind — there may still be time. The abortion pill works in two steps. If only the first pill has been taken, a reversal protocol exists that has helped thousands of women continue their pregnancies.

72 hrs

window to start reversal after taking the first pill

64–68%

success rate when reversal is started within 72 hours (Heartbeat International)

4,500+

babies saved through the Abortion Pill Rescue Network

Abortion Pill Rescue Network (APRN)

Available 24 hours a day. Free. Confidential. No judgment.

Call or Text: 1-877-558-0333

The protocol uses supplemental progesterone — a hormone naturally produced in pregnancy — to counteract the mifepristone. It must be started as soon as possible. Don't wait. Call now.

Abortion Statistics

Prior to Dobbs, Mississippi reported approximately 2,500–3,000 abortions annually. Following the activation of the state's trigger ban, in-state clinical abortion effectively ceased. Medication abortion obtained out-of-state or by mail remains a gray area in ongoing legal battles.

Section 07

Healing After Abortion

If you have had an abortion — recently or years ago — your experience is valid, and your healing matters. Post-abortion trauma is real. It can look like grief, guilt, anxiety, depression, numbness, relationship difficulties, or a quiet ache that never quite left. Many women carry this alone for years. You don't have to.

"The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit."Psalm 34:18

Grace does not have a deadline. Healing is not disqualified by what happened. MLA believes in meeting people in the fullness of their stories — not just the parts that are easy to talk about.

Section 08

What the Research Shows

Breast Cancer & Abortion — an honest look at the conversation

For decades, some researchers and pro-life advocates have raised questions about a potential link between induced abortion and elevated breast cancer risk. The theory centers on breast tissue development: during a first pregnancy, breast tissue undergoes rapid differentiation. Interrupting this process — the reasoning goes — may leave more undifferentiated cells that are vulnerable to becoming cancerous.

Major medical organizations — including the National Cancer Institute, the American Cancer Society, and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — have reviewed the available evidence and concluded that it does not establish a causal link between induced abortion and breast cancer. Some studies in the peer-reviewed literature have found correlations; others have not. The research is ongoing, and the scientific conversation is not entirely closed.

What we believe you deserve

Women making any decision about pregnancy deserve complete, honest information about all potential health considerations — including ones that are debated. We encourage you to ask your doctor directly, review primary sources, and make decisions with full information and full support. If you'd like help finding resources on this topic, reach out to MLA.
