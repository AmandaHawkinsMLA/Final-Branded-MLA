Mississippi's Maternal Health Crisis.

These numbers have names.

The United States leads all high-income nations in maternal mortality. Mississippi leads the United States. This is not a statistic. This is a crisis — and it demands a faithful response.

The National Picture

America is losing mothers.

Maternal mortality refers to deaths occurring during pregnancy or within 42 days of the end of pregnancy, from causes related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management. The United States spends more on healthcare than any other nation on earth — and has the worst maternal mortality rate among high-income countries.

Mississippi Specifically

Our state. Our responsibility.

Mississippi consistently ranks among the states with the highest maternal mortality rates in the country. The causes are interconnected: limited access to prenatal care, high rates of chronic conditions like hypertension and diabetes, geographic barriers to care, and significant racial disparities in how women are treated in the healthcare system.

Access Barriers

Mississippi has a significant shortage of OB-GYNs, particularly in rural counties. Many women in Mississippi travel 60+ miles to reach a maternal care provider. Several counties are entirely without obstetric care.

Chronic Conditions

Mississippi leads the nation in rates of obesity, hypertension, and Type 2 diabetes — all of which significantly elevate the risk of pregnancy complications and maternal death.

Racial Disparities

Black women in Mississippi face compounded risk: they are more likely to have underlying health conditions, less likely to have adequate prenatal care, and face documented disparities in how their pain and symptoms are assessed by providers.

Postpartum Gaps

The majority of pregnancy-related deaths occur after birth — up to one year postpartum. Mississippi's Medicaid coverage historically ended 60 days post-delivery, leaving many women without care during the most dangerous period.

What MLA Is Doing

Showing up in the gap.

Mississippi Life Alliance's Porchlight Initiative and Wellness Baskets directly address the practical gaps that make pregnancy more dangerous for Mississippi families. When a woman has diapers, formula, postpartum supplies, and a community around her, she is more likely to access care — and more likely to survive.

Practical supplies for expectant mothers — vitamins, hygiene items, infant essentials, and connection to community care. Dignity in a box.
