Child Welfare Statistics.

Every child deserves to be seen.

Foster care numbers, maltreatment data, and the landscape of child welfare in Mississippi and across the country. Numbers that demand a response from the community of faith.

What Happens When Youth Age Out

The cost of not having a family.

When a youth ages out of foster care without being adopted or placed in a permanent home, the outcomes are stark. These statistics reflect what happens when children grow up without the safety net of family.

1 in 5

youth who age out of foster care experience homelessness before age 26

50%

of aged-out youth struggle with substance abuse within two years of leaving care

25%

of youth who age out of foster care experience incarceration within two years

<3%

of aged-out foster youth earn a college degree (compared to 28% of the general population)

These are not inevitable outcomes. They are the predictable result of children growing up without consistent, committed adult relationships. Every statistic represents a person. And every person is reachable — especially by a community willing to show up.

Mississippi

Child welfare in our state.

Mississippi's child welfare system is overseen by the Department of Child Protection Services (DCPS), which was restructured and separated from the Department of Human Services in 2016 following years of federal oversight and consent decrees related to systemic failures in the foster care system.

The Foster Care Need

Mississippi has a persistent need for licensed foster families, particularly in rural areas. There is a specific need for families willing to accept sibling groups, children with medical needs, and teens.

Family Preservation

When safe and appropriate, Mississippi prioritizes family reunification. DCPS offers services to help biological families stabilize before removal, and reunification services after placement.

Kinship Care

A significant portion of Mississippi children in foster care are placed with relatives — grandparents, aunts, uncles. Kinship caregivers often need support, training, and resources to meet these children's needs.

MLA's Rooted Initiative exists specifically to support foster and adoptive families in Mississippi. If you are fostering, adopting, or considering either — we want to walk alongside you.

The Faith Community's Role

If every church in Mississippi did one thing.

Research suggests there are more than 6,300 churches in Mississippi. If every church provided support to just one foster or adoptive family, the shortage of foster homes would be eliminated overnight. The system does not need more policies. It needs more people.
